## The lecture in one minute

- **The Cultural Revolution (1966–1976)** began as **a power struggle** between **Mao Zedong** and other top leaders, above all **Liu Shaoqi**.
- Its roots go back to the failure of the **Great Leap Forward (1958–60)**.
- Mao mobilised **university and high school students** as **Red Guards**. They attacked "enemies" with **big-character posters (*dazibao*)** and smashed the "**Four Olds**".
- The art of the period falls into three groups:

| Group | What it shows | Key examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Red Guard art** | Red Guards attacking enemies and old things | Woodcut "Revolution is no Crime, Rebellion is Justified" (1966); *Scatter the old world, build a new world* (1967); *Fully criticize the Chinese Khrushchev* (1967) |
| **Maoism: Mao as an icon** | The leader as hero | Poster from the 1925 photo; *Chairman Mao Goes to Anyuan* (1968); the Yangzi swim images (1966–1972) |
| **Worker-Peasant-Soldier art** | Ordinary people as heroes, especially **women** | *We are proud…* (1954); *Women hold up half the sky* (1974); *Lofty Aspirations Touch the Clouds* (1973); *New Barefoot Doctor in the Fishing Village* (1974) |

- **Conclusion — the role of art and visual materials:**
  1. "**mobilizing the viewer's emotion and their social actions**"
  2. "**glorifying individuals, leaders, and ordinary people**"
  3. "**imagining an idealistic society**"

## Part 1 — What was the Cultural Revolution? (slides 2–7) ★★

### Definition
- "**The Cultural Revolution (1966–1976)**."
- "Other names: The Great **Cultural Revolution**; The Great **Proletarian** Cultural Revolution."
- "A complex **social upheaval** mobilized among **university and high school students**, calling for '**continuing revolution**' by engaging in **revolutionary struggle**."
- "**Educated urban youth** sent down to the countryside, engaging in manual labor."
- She said: it lasted roughly 10 years, and **the first years were the most intense**.

### How it started
- "Began as a **political struggle** between **Chairman Mao** and other top party leaders for dominance of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP), spread to all walks of life across the country."
- The two faces on the slide: "Chairman **Mao Zedong**" and "(Vice) Chairman **Liu Shaoqi**".

### The chain of events ★★ (this is the story to learn)

| Step | What happened | Slide wording |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Great Leap Forward (1958–60)**, launched by Mao | "A radical socio-economic movement". "**Industrialization** of people's work (machines, tractors, tools)". "**Collectivization** of people's life (land + labor + time)". Aim: "to expedite agricultural and industrial productions" |
| | Its slogan | "**Greater, faster, better, and more economical!**" |
| 2 | It fails | "→ **a catastrophe (great famine, >30 million people died)**" |
| 3 | Early 1960s: Mao steps back | "Chairman Mao Zedong **stepped down** from governing the country" |
| 4 | Liu Shaoqi and Zhou Enlai take over | "Vice-Chairman **Liu Shaoqi** and Premier **Zhou Enlai** **stepped up**": "more practical, moderate leaders" who "introduced more **conservative socio-economic policies** based on **individual incentives**", "e.g. allowing families to farm their own plots of land" |
| 5 | The economy recovers | "→ China's economy grew steadily from **1962 to 1965**" |
| 6 | Mao objects | "Mao detested such policies; sign of **private ownership**", "**against the principles of pure communism**" |
| 7 | Mao strikes back | "**Launched CR in 1966**" |

**In plain words:** Mao's big plan caused a famine, so he lost control. The men who replaced him fixed the economy by letting families farm for themselves. Mao saw that as a step towards private ownership, and he used students to attack those leaders and win power back.

The photos on the Great Leap slide: people marching to the fields in a column and digging together ("**Collectively engaging in agricultural production**"), and a packed canteen ("**Collectively eating**").

## Part 2 — How the movement worked (slides 8–14) ★★

### Red Guards
- "**Schools and universities were closed in autumn and winter 1966.**"
- "**University and high school students** formed into groups of militant units — the front line of the new revolutionary upheaval; **militarization**."
- "**Armbands** were distributed to students", "by radical members of the leadership", "declaring the students to be 'Red Guards' who could participate in 'revolutionary struggle'". "Range: moderate to ultra-patriotic."
- "**Little Red Book**: a collection of Mao's sayings for red guards to study and follow."
- Photo: Red Guards waving the Little Red Book in a Beijing parade, **June 1966**.

**The Red Guard kit, as shown:** green army-style uniform + red armband + Little Red Book. Learn it, because it is how you recognise a Red Guard in every poster.

### Big-character posters (*dazibao*) ★★
- "A **handwritten** big poster used **to attack targeted enemies** (higher administrations at schools and government offices)."
- "**Began with Peking University**"; "spread to other universities, high schools, and government offices in China".
- Photos: students at Beijing Normal University writing posters "**to denounce Liu Shaoqi**"; Jiangsu provincial government staff making them in 1967.
- She said: "Remember the name of this? *Da zi bao*, big character poster."

### Attacks and purges
- "**To attack authority figures, verbally & physically**: communist party members; teachers, school administrators; neighbors; even their friends, relatives, and parents."
- "For **wrongdoings**, past or present": "**'rightist' thinking**, experience, and connections"; "**'counter-revolutionary'** behavior or actions".
  - She said: having relatives overseas, for example in Hong Kong, could make you suspected of rightist thinking.
- "At the same time, **purges** were carried out in the high ranks of the Communist Party."
- "**Binary thinking: 'You cross out the bad person and leave the good person.'**"
- Two examples, each photographed with a placard on which the name is **crossed out**:
  - **Peng Zhen** — "top member of CCP; purged for **opposing Mao's beliefs regarding the role of literature in relation to the state**".
  - **Lu Dingyi** — "top member of CCP; purged for **generating conflicts within the party**".

### "Politically purged" — the link back to Dong Xiwen ★★
The slide puts two versions of **Dong Xiwen, *Inaugural Ceremony for the New Nation*** side by side:

| | Original version, **1952–53** | 3rd version, **1967** |
|---|---|---|
| **Gao Gang** — "purged in **1954–1955**" | Present (far right, in glasses) | Gone |
| **Liu Shaoqi** — "purged in **1967**" | Present | Gone |

- She said: Liu Shaoqi "was purged or removed from the office at the beginning of the Cultural Revolution, so that's why he was erased from the painting".

**In plain words:** when a leader fell, he was painted out. "Cross out the bad person" was done even to a famous painting. (This painting is the centre of the Week 8 lecture.)

### "To shatter objects": the Four Olds ★★
- "**The 'Four Olds': old customs, old habits, old culture, and old thinking.**"
- Targets: "Many temples, valuable works of art, and buildings in China."
- Photo: "A buddha statue at **Lin Yin Temple in Hangzhou**… covered with signs reading: '**Destroy the old world**'; '**Establish a new world**'; '**Long live! The proletariat in power!**'" A second photo shows religious statues on a bonfire.

## Part 3 — Red Guard art (slides 15–23) ★★

### 1. Propaganda poster for the People's Liberation Army
- "Red Army and Red Guard members holding Mao Zedong's Little Red Book and weapons, **charging forward**."

### 2. Woodcut Red Guard poster (1966)
- Slogan: "**Revolution is no Crime, Rebellion is Justified.**"
- **What you see:** a uniformed figure with an armband, legs wide apart, shouting, holding up a book of Mao's writings.
- She said: slogans and images like this **emboldened** young students to join the class struggle.

### 3. Model Opera
- A performer in uniform and red armband raises a pistol high, fist clenched, eyes wide.
- Slide: "**Poses and hand gestures modified from Peking Opera.**"
- She said: actors took the forceful gestures of traditional Peking opera and turned them into political gestures. That is why model operas look so powerful: they stand on an older cultural foundation.

**Notice the irony:** a movement that smashed "old culture" built its new stage art on old Peking Opera.

### 4. *Scatter the old world, build a new world* 打碎舊世界創立新世界 ★★ (she spent the longest on this)

| | |
|---|---|
| Made by | "**Central Art Academy Woodblock Print Combat Group**" |
| Published | "Beijing: People's Art Publishing, **April 1967**" |
| Medium | A **woodcut** image, mass-produced as a poster |

- She said: even art students were organised into production teams to make propaganda for the revolution.

**Reading it, part by part:**

| Part of the poster | What it is | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| The big figure | A **Red Guard**: uniform, cap, armband | The hero of the picture |
| What he holds | A large, exaggerated **sledgehammer**, swinging down | Destroying the old world |
| The heap under his feet | A **crucifix**, a **Buddha statue**, **books**, a **vinyl record**, **dice**, a block carved with the character for "virtue" (德) | The Four Olds. Religion (Chinese Buddhism and Western Christianity alike), old teaching and morality, and "bourgeois" entertainment |
| The background | Crowds with Red Guard flags; a building with a red star; people on ladders at signboards | A city. Red Guards marching in and taking over |

- The slide's notes: "Smashing the 'Four Olds' (religious sculptures, books, and other items) and anything considered '**rightists**' (**bourgeois**)." "Storming buildings and taking down signboards." "Smashed to pieces."
- She said: the record and the dice stand for **bourgeois (middle-class) culture**, which counted as rightist. The background building is a state-run cinema.
- **A cross-lecture point she agreed with in class:** the **Nationalist** government *used* traditional culture to build the nation (Week 4, the Southeast Scenic Tour). The **PRC** in the Cultural Revolution wanted a new culture for the proletariat, so it had to **erase** the old one.

### 5. *Fully criticize the Chinese Khrushchev from a political, ideological and theoretical perspective* (November 1967) ★★

**Who are the four figures?** (her reading in class)

| Figure | How you can tell | Stands for |
|---|---|---|
| A man with a cap, work overalls, a cloth round his neck, holding a giant **brush** | Worker's clothes | The **worker** |
| A man in a straw hat | Farmer's hat | The **peasant** |
| A man with a **red star** on his cap | Army cap | The **soldier** (PLA) |
| A younger figure with a Mao badge on the cap | — | A **student / Red Guard** |

- Worker + peasant + soldier = **the proletarian class**.
- They hold red books of Mao's writings: they act on Mao's teaching.
- **The brush is the weapon.** She said: the worker "is not a painter", but even workers can pick up a brush to write posters and attack political enemies.
- The enemy is tiny, crumbling in the bottom corner.

**Who is the "Chinese Khrushchev"?**

| **Khrushchev** | **Liu Shaoqi** |
|---|---|
| "The Soviet party leader **Stalin's successor**" | "**Mao's successor after the Great Leap Forward**" |
| "**Denounced Stalin's misdeeds**" | "**Betrayed Mao's policies**" |
| "Eventually dismissed and put under house arrest" | "Considered as the '**Chinese Khrushchev**'" |

**In plain words:** Khrushchev followed Stalin and then turned on him. Liu followed Mao and (in Mao's eyes) turned on his policies. So Liu was given Khrushchev's name as an insult.

### 6. "Militarization" — two more posters
- Two posters attacking **Liu Shaoqi and Deng Xiaoping**. In one, a huge worker lunges with a giant pen held like a rifle.
- She said: "**don't worry about these two posters**, I'm showing them here for your reference." Know only the idea: **the pen or brush is drawn as a weapon**.

## Part 4 — Maoism: Mao as an icon (slides 25–33) ★★

### Maoism
- "A **doctrine** composed of the **ideology and methodology for revolution** developed by **Mao Zedong and his associates** in the Chinese Communist Party **from the 1920s until Mao's death in 1976**."
- She said: Mao's image was reproduced across every medium (oil painting, woodcut, Chinese painting, poster, photograph), and through books like the Little Red Book he became an international leader, especially in the Third World.

### Mao as an icon in **poster**
- A black-and-white "**Photo of Mao in Guangzhou in 1925**" beside a colour "**Poster of Mao, 1966–1976**" (British Museum collection) made from it.
- She said: you know it is a propaganda poster because it has **an image and a slogan**. The slogan wishes the great leader Chairman Mao long life. Photographs were edited and coloured in the studio for mass production, in millions of copies.

### Mao as an icon in **history painting**: *Chairman Mao Goes to Anyuan* ★★

| | |
|---|---|
| Artist | "**Liu Cunhua & others**" — this is how her slide spells it; you may also see "Liu Chunhua" |
| Date | **1968** |
| Size | **2.2 × 1.8 m** (the 2.2 is underlined on the slide) |
| What you see | Young Mao in a long grey-blue gown on a hilltop, one fist clenched, an umbrella under his arm; sky and clouds around his head, mountains far below |
| Compared with | **Arkady Rylov, *Lenin in Razliv*** (1934) |

- She said about the size: the figure is meant to be **life-size**, so in front of the painting "you can feel the physical presence of Chairman Mao".
- She said about the comparison: celebrating a leader in history painting "**was learned from the Soviet Union**". Both pictures place the leader on the **central vertical axis**, against a **wild landscape**, seen **from a low viewpoint**, so he looks very tall. The aim is "**to monumentalize**" him, to make him a hero to worship.

**How it was made** (slide 28):
- **Photo of Mao in 1925 → pre-painting study** (a drawing on a squared grid, for accurate copying) **→ oil painting → poster**.
- "**Painting → poster**"; "**Joint collaboration, censorship**"; "**Mass produced, circulated nationwide**."
- She said: art workers after 1949 were coordinated through political organisations, so work passed easily from the art school to the publishing house. Comments were made during the process on how to make Mao look more heroic.

### Mao's 1966 swim in the Yangzi River: one photo, many media ★★

| Step | Slide | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **1966** | "A photograph of Mao, by his **personal full-time photographer** during his **historic 1966 swimming in the Yangzi River**": Mao in a white bathrobe on a boat, arm raised |
| 2 | — | The same photo on a booklet cover with an English slogan |
| 3 | **1967** | "Propaganda poster **reproduced from a woodcut print**", by "Henan 27 Communes": Mao in profile, with a long column of swimmers carrying red flags added |
| 4 | **July 1969** | Colour poster: "**Closely follow the great leader Chairman Mao and forge ahead courageously amid great storms and waves**", celebrating "the **third anniversary**" of the swim; made by the "Shanghai 1969 Swim the Yangzi River headquarters" |
| 5 | **1972** | **Tang Xiaohe** (b. 1941), *Follow Closely Our Great Leader Chairman Mao, Ride the Wind, Cleave the Waves, Fearlessly Forge Ahead*, oil on canvas, 188 × 290 cm |

- **Why the swim mattered.** She said: it was "to demonstrate his good health" when he was in his early seventies.
- **How you spot a woodcut.** She said: a sharp contrast between black outline and empty space.
- **Tang Xiaohe's painting — "Red, bright, and shining" as the official style ★★.** Mao glows with a ruddy face in bright sunlight, surrounded by beaming young people: soldiers, Red Guards, swimmers, a small boy.
  - She said: "it involves a lot of **imagination**". The photograph shows Mao alone. The painter **added** the young people "to show that he was very popular among young people".

**In plain words:** "red, bright, and shining" is the look of official Cultural Revolution painting: warm red faces, strong light, everything glowing and happy.

### Sculpture
- "**Sculpture of Mao in Socialist Realism**": towering statues of Mao with a raised arm, set beside a statue with the same outstretched-arm pose. She said the practice of celebrating a leader in sculpture also came from the Soviet Union, and that the statue is huge compared with the people in the square.

## Part 5 — Worker-Peasant-Soldier art (slides 34–41) ★★

"**Feminism: Liberation of women**"

- She said: traditionally art was made by social elites or showed heroes. This art celebrates **ordinary people**, the masses, as "the masters of the communist age".
- She chose to focus on **how women are shown**.

### Mao's slogan ★★
"**Women hold up half the sky**" 妇女能顶半边天

- She said: women are as powerful as men. They were no longer confined to staying home, minding children and doing housework; they were given the power to work in production like men.

### The works

| Work | Details | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| ***We are proud of participating in the founding of our country's industrialization*** | **Ding Hao**, **February 1954**, Shanghai | A smiling woman **welder**, mask pushed up, in a factory. Slide: "Women are encouraged to work in industrial and technical professions. **Emancipation + the great need for labor.**" |
| ***Women hold up half the sky*** | **Sun Quan**, **November 1974**; second printing, print run 60,001–173,000 | Three women stacked from bottom to top: two **workers** with tools (wrench, hammer, rope) and a **pilot** in helmet and goggles |
| **Oil painting learned from Soviet socialist realism** | "**Konstantin M. Maksimov** (1913–93): **taught in Beijing in the mid-late 1950s**, influenced Chinese painter students." "Painting **model workers, peasants and soldiers**" | Chinese and Soviet paintings of working women side by side |
| ***Lofty Aspirations Touch the Clouds*** 壯志凌雲 | **Yuan Hao**, **1973** | A woman sitting on a **high-voltage cable** high above fields and a river, smiling |
| ***New Barefoot Doctor in the Fishing Village*** | **Chen Yanning**, **1974**; heading "**Sent-down youth**" | A smiling barefoot young woman on a fishing boat with a medical bag and a bucket marked with a red cross |

**More on each (what she said):**

- **1954 welder poster.** Two reasons sit behind it: women were **emancipated** (liberated), and China **needed a lot of labour**. Note the date: **1954**, well before the Cultural Revolution.
- ***Women hold up half the sky* (1974).** The three women form a **monumental group** rising from the bottom of the poster to the sky. They fill **more than half** of the picture, so the image itself "proves" the slogan. The second printing alone passed 100,000 copies.
- **Maksimov slide.** One painting shows a woman **party leader** with a Mao badge, placed on the central vertical axis, healthy and leader-like. The practice of celebrating the proletarian in oil painting was learned from the Soviet Union.
- ***Lofty Aspirations Touch the Clouds*.** She has climbed very high with almost **no protective gear**, and she looks **confident, looking up, smiling**. The land far below shows how high she is and so how brave. It echoes "women hold up half the sky". It is compared with a Soviet-style picture of the same subject and with a photographic poster of two men on a pylon. She said the painted woman looks **even more heroic** than the men: she is **alone** and has no supporting structure round her.
- ***New Barefoot Doctor*.** A **sent-down youth**: a city girl of about 16 or 17, given some medical training and sent to a village. The **red cross** shows her medical role. **No shoes** = "barefoot doctor". She is handing out herbal tea to the fishermen. Although the work is hard she looks **happy and healthy**: red face, light on her sweaty forehead.

**The common look:** happy, healthy, strong, red-cheeked, lit by bright light. It is the same "red, bright, and shining" style used for Mao, now used for ordinary people.

## Conclusion (slide 42) ★★

"**The role of art and visual materials**":
1. "**mobilizing the viewer's emotion and their social actions**"
2. "**glorifying individuals, leaders, and ordinary people**"
3. "**imagining an idealistic society**"

She said: woodcut prints, paintings, posters, photographs and big-character posters together formed a vibrant **visual culture** that mobilised people from the city to the village. Looking at them, "you feel like, wow, you are empowered". Together they imagine an ideal communist society.

**Match the three roles to the three kinds of art:**

| Role | Best example |
|---|---|
| Mobilising emotion and action | Red Guard art, big-character posters |
| Glorifying leaders | Mao as an icon |
| Glorifying ordinary people | Worker-Peasant-Soldier art |
| Imagining an ideal society | All of them: everyone is healthy, happy and united |

## Easy to mix up

| Do not confuse | With |
|---|---|
| **Great Leap Forward**: 1958–60, economic movement, famine | **Cultural Revolution**: 1966–1976, political and social upheaval |
| Slogan "**Greater, faster, better, and more economical**" (Great Leap) | Slogan "**Revolution is no crime, rebellion is justified**" (Red Guards) |
| **Mao**: stepped down in the early 1960s | **Liu Shaoqi and Zhou Enlai**: stepped up |
| **Liu Shaoqi**: the "Chinese Khrushchev"; purged **1967** | **Gao Gang**: purged **1954–55** (before the Cultural Revolution) |
| **Peng Zhen**: purged over the role of literature and the state | **Lu Dingyi**: purged for generating conflicts within the party |
| ***Dazibao***: **handwritten** posters attacking a named enemy | **Propaganda posters**: printed image + slogan, mass-produced |
| **Four Olds**: customs, habits, culture, thinking | The poster's heap: crucifix, Buddha, books, record, dice |
| ***Chairman Mao Goes to Anyuan*** (1968): young Mao, from a **1925 photo**; compared with **Rylov's Lenin** | **Tang Xiaohe's swim painting** (1972): old Mao, from the **1966 photo**; "red, bright, and shining" |
| Swim: **1966**. Woodcut poster: **1967**. Third-anniversary poster: **1969**. Oil painting: **1972** | Anyuan painting: **1968** |
| *We are proud…* (**1954**, Ding Hao, one welder) | *Women hold up half the sky* (**1974**, Sun Quan, three women) |
| **Yuan Hao**: woman on a high-voltage cable (1973) | **Chen Yanning**: barefoot doctor on a fishing boat (1974) |
| **Maksimov**: Soviet painter who taught in Beijing, mid–late 1950s | **Rylov**: Soviet painter of *Lenin in Razliv* (1934) |
| **Model Opera**: borrows poses from Peking Opera | The **Four Olds** campaign: destroys old culture |
| Nationalists (Week 4): **used** tradition to build the nation | Cultural Revolution: **erased** tradition to build a new world |
