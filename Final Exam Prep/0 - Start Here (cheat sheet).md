## How she writes questions

## Lookalike pairs — do not swap these

| Pair | First | Second |
|---|---|---|
| **Xu Beihong vs Lin Fengmian** | Academic realism: anatomy, chiaroscuro, three-dimensionality; plaster casts, live models, still life; "art for social and political sake" | Post-Impressionism, Fauvism, German Expressionism; liberation of forms, color, lines; "art for art's sake" |
| **Kang Youwei vs Cai Yuanpei** | Political reformer; relics conservation + theory of realism (Tang–Song best) | Educator; aesthetic education replaces religion; Minister of Education, Peking University chancellor |
| **Xugu vs Ren Xiong vs Ren Yi** | Xugu: monk name, Zen negative space, cats/goldfish/fruit | Ren Xiong: self-portrait, cinnabar, gold leaf, Magu. Ren Yi (Ren Bonian): satirical portraits of Gao Yongzhi and Wu Changshi |
| **Shanghai School vs yuefenpai** | Paintings sold via dealers and shops to merchant collectors, 19th c. | Printed calendar posters with pretty ladies + commodities, in Sunday papers, 1910s–30s |
| **Guohua vs xihua** | "National painting": ink and brush | Western painting: oil etc. |
| **Storm Society vs Woodcut Movement** | Oil painting; art for art's sake; pure formal world; has a manifesto (1932); Westernised | Prints; social struggle; underrepresented groups; no manifesto; left-wing; artists arrested |
| **Ni Yide vs Pang Xunqin** | Trained in Japan (twice) | Trained in Paris; collage, Surrealism; *Such is Paris*, *Son of the Earth* |
| **Huang Junbi vs Jiang Zhaohe vs Zhang Shuqi** | Huang: *Bombing of Chongqing*, landscape as reportage (Nationalist area) | Jiang: *Refugees*, ink + European realism (Japanese-occupied Beijing). Zhang: *Messengers of World Peace*, diplomatic gift to USA |
| **Zhang Daqian vs Zhang Ding** | Copied 276 Dunhuang murals, 1941–43 | 1979 *Nezha* airport mural in a Dunhuang-derived decorative style |
| **Realism vs socialist realism vs New Realism** | Realism: believable representation (Kang, Xu) | Socialist realism: realistic *and idealised*, proletarian, typical, serves the state. New Realism / Scar Art: realistic and *unidealised* hardship (post-Mao) |
| **Propaganda poster vs dazibao** | Printed image + slogan; medium from the Soviet Union | Handwritten big characters; attacks named enemies; Cultural Revolution |
| **Gao Gang vs Liu Shaoqi** | Purged 1954–55; erased first from *Founding Ceremony* | Purged 1967; "Chinese Khrushchev"; erased in the 1967 third version; later restored |
| **Official vs unofficial art (1979)** | State-commissioned: Beijing airport murals (Zhang Ding, Yuan Yunsheng) | Not state-approved: Stars Group (Wang Keping) |
| **Zhang Ding vs Yuan Yunsheng** | *Nezha Conquers the Dragon King*; mythology; accepted | *Water Splashing Festival*; Dai minority; Modigliani-like figures; **nudes → controversy** |
| **Luo Zhongli vs Guang Tingbo** | *Father* (1980): peasant | *Molten Steel & Sweat* (1981): steelworker |
| **Zhang Hongtu vs Wang Guangyi** | US-based from 1982; plays with **Mao's face**; *The Last Banquet* | China-based; **propaganda figures + brand logos**; *Great Criticism* series |
| **Xu Bing vs Cai Guoqiang** | Text and printmaking: *Book from the Sky*, *Square Word Calligraphy* | Gunpowder and explosions: *Project for Extraterrestrials* |
| **Modernization vs modernity** | The *process* of transforming (rural/agrarian → urban/industrial) | The *quality or state* of being or appearing modern |

## Threads that run across lectures

- **Realism:** Kang's theory (Wk 3) → Xu Beihong (Wk 5) → Jiang Zhaohe's *Refugees* (Wk 7) → socialist realism (Wk 8, 10) → New Realism turns it upside down (Wk 11).
- **Woodcut:** Lu Xun's movement (Wk 6) → wartime Guilin (Wk 7) → Red Guard prints (Wk 10) → Stars exhibition (Wk 11) → Xu Bing's printmaking training (Wk 12).
- **Mao's image:** *Founding Ceremony* (Wk 8) → cult images, *Anyuan*, Yangtze swim (Wk 10) → Wang Keping's *Idol* (Wk 11) → Warhol, Zhang Hongtu, Vivienne Tam (Wk 12).
- **Steel:** "We and ore together make steel!" 1959 poster (Wk 8) is the direct comparison for *Molten Steel & Sweat* 1981 (Wk 11).
- **Tradition as a political tool:** Nationalists *revive* tradition (guohua, cultural nationalism, Wk 4) while the Cultural Revolution *smashes* it (Four Olds, Wk 10).
- **The nude:** Lin Fengmian's *Nude* 1934 = the West, the modern, freedom (Wk 5); Yuan Yunsheng's 1979 nudes show where freedom stopped (Wk 11).
- **Art for art's sake:** Lin Fengmian (Wk 5) and the Storm Society (Wk 6) versus Xu Beihong, the woodcut artists and all Mao-era art.

## One-line glossary (slide wording)

- **Patronage** — financial or other support given to an artist or organisation.
- **Haipai / Shanghai School** — artists active in Shanghai, not born there, who relocated to make a living.
- **Modernization** — transformation from a traditional, rural, agrarian society to a secular, urban, industrial one.
- **Modernity** — the quality or state of being or appearing modern; a break from the past through industrialization, urbanization and technology.
- **Institutionalization** — the process by which something becomes established, organised and embedded in a formal institution or system.
- **Realism** — representing people, places and things in a believable and recognisable way (form, mass and volume, proportion, shading, perspective).
- **Aesthetic education (meiyu)** — Cai Yuanpei's programme: love of beauty frees minds from greed, prejudice, selfishness and hatred.
- **Nationalism** — loyalty and devotion to the nation-state surpass other individual or group interests.
- **Cultural nationalism** — mobilising culture to construct a national identity.
- **Guohua** — "national painting": ink-and-brush painting nationalised as *the* Chinese painting.
- **Chiaroscuro** — contrast of light and dark to model form.
- **Avant-garde** — "front guard": iconoclastic, individualistic, self-expressive, idealistic.
- **Zeitgeist** — the spirit of an era.
- **Politics in command** — art must serve political purposes (Yan'an 1942; nationwide after 1949).
- **Socialist realism** — an ideological guidance (not just a style): realistic, proletarian subjects, typical scenes, idealistic; to exemplify, educate, instil hope.
- **Sinicization** — making an imported medium (oil painting) look and feel Chinese.
- **Iconography** — identifying figures by their features, clothing and associated motifs.
- **Purge** — removal of unwanted figures from the Party and office.
- **Red Guards** — student militant units of the Cultural Revolution.
- **Dazibao** — handwritten big-character poster attacking targeted enemies.
- **Four Olds** — old customs, old habits, old culture, old thinking.
- **Maoism** — doctrine of ideology *and* methodology for revolution developed by Mao and associates, 1920s–1976.
- **Rehabilitated / exonerated** — a purged person's label is removed and position restored.
- **Official / unofficial art** — state-commissioned and approved, versus made and shown outside state approval.
- **New Realism / Scar Art** — post-Mao realist painting showing real hardship without glorification.
- **Globalization** — free flow of capital, human agents and ideas; a borderless world; a global village.
- **Political Pop** — political icons + pop art; ridicules Mao's revolutionary legacy.
- **Paper tiger** — looks terrifying, is in fact powerless; Mao's term (since 1946) for the USA and American imperialism.
- **Demystification** — removing the mythical power attached to something.
