## Part 1 — Relationship marketing and the product (slides 1–3)

### Slide 1 — Title

### Slide 2 — Relationship marketing and CRM ★★

★★ because the mid-term past paper asked: *A stable customer relationship can be termed as* → Relational.

Slide wording: "Business exchanges." Two types are listed:

- "Discrete Functional Unemotional (Shoelaces, Meat Sauce and Office Supplies)."
- "Continuous Relational Emotional (Professional Services, Sports Teams and Luxury Products)."

The picture on the slide is one line with an arrow at each end:

| Position on the line | Label on the picture | Examples on the picture |
|---|---|---|
| Left end | "Discrete Functional Unemotional" | Shoelaces, Meat Sauce, Office Supplies |
| Middle | (no label) | Hotels, Airlines, Automobiles |
| Right end | "Continuous Relational Emotional" | Professional Services, Sports Teams, Luxury Products |

Under the line, a second arrow points to the right. Its label is "More benefit from relationship marketing".

**In plain words:**

- Left end: you buy once, you buy only to use the thing, and you do not care who sells it.
- Right end: you keep buying over time, you know the seller, and feelings are part of it (think of a fan and a sports team).
- The further right an exchange is, the more a company gains from relationship marketing.
- Slide 4 calls the left end "the transactional end of the continuum" and the right end "the relationship end of the continuum". So a stable, lasting customer relationship is relational. A one-off deal is transactional.

Note: "Hotels, Airlines, Automobiles" are only in the picture, not in the bullet text. They are the middle cases.

### Slide 3 — Extended Product Model ★

★ because it has an exact definition sentence and a labelled diagram, which suit MCQ and a short definition question.

Slide wording: "The Extended Product Model states that a product is much more than simply its physical characteristics. It consists of maintenance, service, installation, training, warranties, delivery, and image, all of which offer companies the opportunities for creating value for the customer over time."

The diagram is three circles, one inside the other:

| Ring | Name on the diagram | Items on the diagram |
|---|---|---|
| Inner | "Core component" | Product platform; Design features; Functional features |
| Middle | "Packaging component" | Price; Quality; Package; Styling; Trademark; Brand name; Legal |
| Outer | "Support services component" | Repair and maintenance; Installation; Instructions; Other related services; Deliveries; Warranty; Spare parts; Legal |

**In plain words:** The customer does not buy only the physical thing. The customer also buys the brand and price around it, and the services after the sale. Each ring gives the company another chance to create value "over time". That is why this model belongs in a CRM lecture.

Traps in the diagram:

- "Legal" appears in two rings: packaging and support services.
- "Warranty", "Deliveries" and "Installation" are support services, not packaging.
- "Price", "Quality" and "Styling" are packaging, not core.

Example **(extra, not on the slides)**: a new air-conditioner. Core = it cools the room. Packaging = its brand name and price. Support services = installation, warranty, repair.

## Part 2 — Three levels of relationship marketing and what a relationship is (slides 4–5)

### Slide 4 — Three levels of relationship marketing ★★

★★ because it is prime MCQ and True/False material: three items whose bond type and target are easy to swap. It also gives the words "transactional end" and "relationship end".

Slide wording, numbered as on the slide:

1. "Level one relationship marketing relies primarily on pricing incentives and is most appropriately aimed at customers toward the transactional end of the continuum."
2. "Level two relationship marketing relies primarily on social bonds involving personalization and customization aimed at customers toward the relationship end of the continuum."
3. "Level three relationship marketing is also aimed at customers toward the relationship end of the continuum, but bonds are established by structural solutions as opposed to the personalized, such as with computer terminals and software to track their orders (FedEx), imaging equipment to customize clothing."

| Level | The bond comes from | Aimed at | In plain words |
|---|---|---|---|
| One | "pricing incentives" | "the transactional end" | The customer stays because of price. |
| Two | "social bonds involving personalization and customization" | "the relationship end" | The customer stays because of people and personal service. |
| Three | "structural solutions as opposed to the personalized" | "also ... the relationship end" | The customer stays because the company's system or equipment is built into the customer's work. |

Traps:

- Only level one aims at the transactional end. Levels two and three BOTH aim at the relationship end.
- Level two = social (people). Level three = structural (systems, equipment).
- The level three example "imaging equipment to customize clothing" contains the word "customize". It is still level three, because the bond is the equipment.
- FedEx belongs to level three: "computer terminals and software to track their orders".

Example **(extra, not on the slides)**: Level one: a shop gives members a discount. Level two: the shop owner knows your name and your usual order. Level three: a supplier installs its own ordering system in your office.

### Model answers

**Q1 (4 marks). Mid-term B3(b): Propose TWO strategies to prevent downward customer migration and retain high-value customers.**

This is the official marking scheme, 2 marks for each strategy:

1. **Proactive monitoring** (2 marks). Use early-warning systems, based on business intelligence, to detect dissatisfaction signals. Intervene before the customer migrates.
2. **Relationship strengthening** (2 marks). Assign personal bankers to high-value clients. Give preferential treatment (priority service, fee waivers). Build programmatic bonds through exclusive events.

Also accepted by the scheme: cross-selling or up-selling; customization; addressing "moments of truth".

How each point maps to the slides: monitoring → item 15 ("dissatisfaction" is the first reason for downward migration). Personal bankers → item 6, humanistic bonds. Preferential treatment → item 9. Exclusive events → item 5, programmatic bonds. The other accepted answers → items 13, 14, 12 and 8.

Writing tip: for each strategy, first name it, then say how it stops the customer from buying less.

**Q2 (3 marks). 2025-26 final Q25(d): demarket the *Occasional Bargain Hunters*, or try to migrate them? Give TWO arguments.**

No official marking scheme was available for these notes. The split below (1 mark for the recommendation, 1 mark for each argument) follows the exam rule that a correct point usually carries one mark. Demarketing itself is defined in lecture CRM1; take the definition from there, not from this lecture.

1. **Recommendation** (1 mark). Do not demarket the whole segment. Try to migrate the customers with potential into a more profitable segment.
2. **Argument 1: customer selection and migration path** (1 mark). Select first: use "profit drivers as segmentation variables" to find the bargain hunters who can become profitable, because we should "Target only those customers whose retention will be profitable". Then give them a path. They sit at "the transactional end of the continuum", so start with level one "pricing incentives". Next use "Cross-Selling" (bundles found by data mining) and "Up-Selling" ("better and more expensive services and products"). The aim is the "Conversion of Transactional Buyers to Relational Buyers".
3. **Argument 2: long-term profitability and risk** (1 mark). "Migration is the change in customer value over time", so low profit today is not fixed. Managing migration means working on the three reasons customers buy less: "dissatisfaction"; "attraction to a better attribute mix offered by alternative brands"; "change in life stage or life cycle". Demarketing gives up this future profit. It also risks negative word-of-mouth and brand damage. That hurts acquisition, which depends on "word-of-month referrals", and it works against "CRM as Brand-Building Tool".

If the case data show the segment cannot move up, the opposite answer is also defensible: recommend demarketing (definition from CRM1). Argument 1: customer selection, "Target only those customers whose retention will be profitable", so resources go to profitable segments. Argument 2: bargain hunters respond mainly to "pricing incentives" (level one), so a migration path to relational buying is weak; then say how to limit the risk of negative word-of-mouth. Choose ONE side and argue it.

**Q3 (3 marks). Describe the three levels of relationship marketing.**

1. Level one "relies primarily on pricing incentives" and is aimed at customers toward "the transactional end of the continuum". (1 mark)
2. Level two "relies primarily on social bonds involving personalization and customization", aimed at customers toward "the relationship end of the continuum". (1 mark)
3. Level three is also aimed at the relationship end, but "bonds are established by structural solutions as opposed to the personalized", for example "computer terminals and software to track their orders (FedEx)". (1 mark)

**Q4 (4 marks). Define customer migration and give the three reasons for downward migration.**

1. "Migration is the change in customer value over time"; "downward migration characterizes customers who buy less". (1 mark)
2. Reason 1: "dissatisfaction". (1 mark)
3. Reason 2: "attraction to a better attribute mix offered by alternative brands". (1 mark)
4. Reason 3: "change in life stage or life cycle". (1 mark)

**Q5 (3 marks). Explain the Extended Product Model.**

1. It "states that a product is much more than simply its physical characteristics". (1 mark)
2. The product "consists of maintenance, service, installation, training, warranties, delivery, and image". The diagram shows three components: core, packaging and support services. (1 mark)
3. All of these "offer companies the opportunities for creating value for the customer over time". (1 mark)

**Q6 (3 marks). Distinguish acquisition, retention and win-back strategies. Give one slide strategy for each.**

1. Acquisition targets "Prospects who are likely to response to acquisition efforts and become customers". Example: "Mass media advertising". (1 mark)
2. Retention keeps current customers, but "only those customers whose retention will be profitable". Example: "Humanistic bonds (highly trained personnel to look after you!)". (1 mark)
3. Win-back targets "a lost customer". Example: "Analyze the customers price sensitivity and behavior in the last purchases". (1 mark)
