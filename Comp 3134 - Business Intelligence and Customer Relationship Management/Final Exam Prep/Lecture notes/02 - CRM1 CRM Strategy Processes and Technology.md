## The lecture in one minute

- CRM is "a strategic approach" for "creating improved shareholder value" by developing "appropriate relationships with key customers and customer segments". It joins relationship marketing and IT.
- CRM has five key processes: strategy development, value creation, multichannel integration, information management, performance assessment.
- "Not all customers are created equal." Keep and grow the profitable customers. Use de-marketing on the unprofitable ones.
- A CRM system has 10 functions (from "identifying prospects" to "winning back defectors") and 13 summary objectives.
- The basic architecture has five numbered components: front office, BI system, workflow and business rules, physical links to the back office, performance metrics.
- CRM "originated in the mid-to late 1990s". It grew out of market research, relationship marketing, MRP/ERP, sales force automation, campaign management, direct-response marketing and database marketing.
- Implementing CRM is hard because of people and data: six "real barriers", and poor data quality as "the single greatest inhibitor". The six-step timeline is the plan for doing it.
- Customer experience management (CEM) and journey maps plan the customer experience "from end to end" across all touch points.

## Part 1 — What is CRM? (slides 1–3)

**Slide 1** is the title slide ("LECTURE CRM.1").

**Slide 2 ★ (true/false material).** Two statements with arrows pointing to one question in the middle, "How to sell products and services to our customers?":

- "4 Ps (product, price, place, and promotion) : The Foundation of Traditional Marketing Strategy."
- "Customer Relationship Management : The Foundation of Contemporary Marketing Strategy."

**In plain words:** both answer the same question. The old answer: get the product, price, place and promotion right. The new answer: build a relationship with the customer.

**Slide 3 ★★ (asked in the mid-term past paper B1(a) and B1(c)).** Four statements define CRM. Learn the key phrases word for word.

| # | Slide wording | In plain words |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "CRM is a strategic approach that is concerned with creating improved shareholder value through the development of appropriate relationships with key customers and customer segments." | CRM is strategy, not just software. The aim is more value for the owners. The method is the right relationship with the important customers. |
| 2 | "CRM unites the potential of relationship marketing strategies and IT to create profitable, long-term relationships with customers and other key stakeholders." | CRM = relationship marketing + IT. The result is relationships that are profitable and long-term. |
| 3 | "CRM provides enhanced opportunities to use data and information to both understand customers and co-create value with them." | Data helps the company to understand customers and to create value together with them. |
| 4 | "CRM requires a cross-functional integration of processes, people, operations, and marketing capabilities that is enabled through information, technology, and applications." | All departments must work as one. Information, technology and applications make this possible. |

Points to notice:

- "appropriate" relationships: not the same relationship for every customer.
- "key customers and customer segments": CRM chooses whom to serve best.
- Statement 4 lists four things to integrate (processes, people, operations, marketing capabilities) and three enablers (information, technology, applications).

Tiny example **(extra, not on the slides)**: a Hong Kong supermarket chain with a loyalty app. Marketing sends the offers, the shop staff scan the app, customer service answers questions. If the three do not share one customer record, the customer gets three different stories. That is what "cross-functional integration" prevents.

## Part 2 — The five key processes in CRM (slides 4–6)

★★ Asked in the mid-term past paper B1(b): five processes, one mark each. Learn the five names in order.

| # | Process | Slide wording | In plain words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Strategy development | "At both the business level and customer level, with the latter involving decisions regarding the appropriateness of various customer segmentation approaches: mass, segmented, and one-to-one." | Decide the business strategy and the customer strategy. The customer strategy chooses how finely to divide customers. |
| 2 | Value creation | "Involves determining what product/service attributes customers value and which customers and customer segments are valuable to the company. | 4 | Information management | "Involves collecting, organizing, and using customer data and customer information from all touch points (any point of contact that a customer or prospect has with the company) in order to learn more about each customer and generate the appropriate marketing response." | Collect, organize and use data from every contact, to know each customer and to respond in the right way. |

- Process 3 lists the channels (slide 5): "e-mail, mobile, social media, sales personnel, outlets, customer engagement centers (formerly called call centers or customer contact centers), direct marketing, e-commerce, m-commerce, chat, etc."
- Process 4 has three verbs: "collecting, organizing, and using".
- Process 5 has six metrics: "acquisition, retention, win-back, satisfaction, loyalty, and profits".

## Part 3 — Relationship, profitable customers and de-marketing (slides 7–12)

**Slide 7 ★ — Relationship.** All seven bullets:

1. "Relationship is worth more than transactions, e.g., understanding, attention, listening, quick responses, trust, reliability, politeness, knowledge about me, caring about me, remember me, motivate me and create value for me."
2. "Upgrade of services based on previous spending."
3. "Special discounts on birthday, occasion, and for surprise events."
4. "Intelligent questioning and answering."
5. "Provide helps, suggestions and advices to me for making better decisions."
6. "Feeling of customer oriented organizational culture."
7. "Develop Relationship require resources – Needs a strategy to develop relationship with mainly profitable customers."

**In plain words:** a relationship gives the customer more than a sale does. But relationships cost money, so the company builds them "with mainly profitable customers".

**Slide 8.** Video placeholder: Video1-1, topic "Relationship Marketing".

**Slide 9 ★★ — Profitable customers (de-marketing asked in the mid-term MCQ and B2(d)).** All bullets:

- "Not all customers are created equal."
- "Maximize revenue and profitability? Business point of view."
- "Prioritization."
- "Recognize the best customers and retain them, encourage greater usage of product and service, “trade” them up to more prestigious and expensive items."
- "Demand weak – develop plans and programs to increase it."
- "Demand strong – de-marketing, attempts to weaken or extinguish demand when they are unprofitable for the organization. E.g., minimum order sizes, high admittance charges, more charges for lack of profitable usage."
- "Relationship Management – maintaining profitable relationship, de-marketing (terminating) unprofitable relationship."

**In plain words:** rank the customers. For the best ones: keep them, make them use more, move them up to dearer products. For unprofitable ones: make it harder or more expensive to buy, or end the relationship.

The three de-marketing examples on slide 9:

| Example on the slide | In plain words |
|---|---|
| "minimum order sizes" | Very small orders are refused. |
| "high admittance charges" | A high entry fee keeps low-value customers out. |
| "more charges for lack of profitable usage" | Customers who do not use the service in a profitable way pay extra. |

Tiny example **(extra, not on the slides)**: a bank that charges a monthly fee when the account balance is below a set amount is using the third example.

**Slide 10.** Video placeholder: Video1-2, topic "What is Demarketing?".

**Slide 11 ★★ — What is demarketing?**

Reasons (three, as listed on the slide):

1. "Reduce demand due to product shortages."
2. "High advertising costs."
3. "Increase product demands."

Approaches (three):

| Approach | Slide wording | In plain words |
|---|---|---|
| Selective Demarketing | "companies try to reduce demand from specific consumer groups, to protect their well / targeted customers." | Push away *some* groups to protect the customers you want. |
| Ostensible Demarketing | "when brands create artificial shortage of a product to increase the demand, restriction of sales, exclusive sales by invitations,..etc" | Pretend the product is hard to get, so people want it more. |
| General Demarketing | "a company wants to demarket its products for everyone, e.g., Government demarketing alcohol and tobacco products for people's well-being, Patagonia's demarketing strategy was to bring people closer to the environmental impact of consumer behaviour." | Reduce demand from *everybody*. |

**In plain words:** reason 3 looks strange, but it matches ostensible demarketing: a false shortage makes demand go up.

**Slide 12 ★ — Customers that companies do not like.** All twelve items:

1. "Customer who buy infrequently or only buy on big sales."
2. "Customers frequently return merchandise. (After trying, using and return for refund.)"
3. "Customers who complain often."
4. "Customers do not value relationship only are transactional focused."
5. "Customers don't pay on time for rented services, or don't pay at all."
6. "Customers don't accept / recognize the service / product's value."
7. "Customers have unclear and changing demand and requests."
8. "Customers want all your attention (unreasonable demand of all services)."
9. "Customers ignore the required responses / information."
10. "Customers aren't honest and telling the truth about the service / products."
11. "Customers using “force” and “threatening” style of interaction."
12. "Customers don't listen but continuously demanding the same / similar things."

The slide ends with "….etc". **In plain words:** these customers cost more than they bring in. They are the targets of de-marketing.

## Part 4 — Functions and objectives of CRM systems (slides 13–14)

**Slide 13 ★ — Functions of CRM systems (list for MCQ / true-false).** Ten functions, with the note in brackets from the slide:

1. "Identifying prospects; (profitable customers)"
2. "Acquiring customers; (attract, invite, ask them to try, new entrants….etc)"
3. "Developing customers; (relationships)"
4. "Cross-selling; (similar products, or other related / non related products)"
5. "Up-selling; (more expensive, and prestige products)"
6. "Managing migration; (within different customers segments, e.g., from youth to adult)"
7. "Servicing; (intelligent answers, shorter times, better solutions."
8. "Retaining; (long term customers)"
9. "Increasing loyalty; (Samsung, iPhone or Hauwei, i.e., phones, watches, tablets, headphones and computers)"
10. "Winning back defectors. (compensation with discounts, and upgrades)"

**In plain words:** the list follows the life of a customer. Find him, get him, grow him, sell him other things and better things, keep him when he changes segment, serve him, keep him, make him loyal, and get him back if he leaves. The loyalty example means a customer who buys the phone, watch, tablet, headphones and computer all from one brand.

**Slide 14 ★ — Summary objectives of CRM (list for MCQ / true-false).** Thirteen objectives:

1. "identifying potential customers;"
2. "understanding customer needs, both current and latent;"
3. "differentiating profitable from unprofitable customers and segments;"
4. "decreasing attrition by increasing value and satisfaction;"
5. "increasing usage of current products and services;"
6. "increasing usage of a greater number of a company's products and services;"
7. "increasing usage of more prestigious items produced by a company (trading up);"
8. "increasing customer service and satisfaction;"
9. "improving campaign management;"
10. "increasing referrals;"
11. "winning back lost customers;"
12. "moving customers up the relationship hierarchy from strangers to acquaintances to friends to partners;"
13. "integrating marketing and sales efforts throughout the various channels used by the company."

**In plain words:**

- "latent" needs (objective 2) are needs the customer has but has not said yet. **(extra, not on the slides)**
- Objective 6 is cross-selling. Objective 7 is up-selling ("trading up").
- Learn the order of the relationship hierarchy: strangers → acquaintances → friends → partners.

## Part 8 — Organizations' experiences, barriers and communicating the benefits (slides 31–35)

**Slide 31 ★ — Organizations' experiences with CRM (used for the 2024-25 final Q21).** Five types of organization, in slide order:

1. "Manufacturers that have no contact with final customers at point-of-sale and focus on customer relationships with middlemen in the channel."
2. "Transaction-oriented companies that focus on selling more products, and more expensive products, to their existing customer base through a better mix of the 4 Ps (product, price, place, and promotion)."
3. "Companies that practice market segmentation and develop promotional and product strategies at homogeneous groups as opposed to individuals."
4. "Companies that market effectively to their individual customers because they know them and their buying habits and are able to retain them through effective one-on-one retention strategies."
5. "Companies that collaborate with individual customers in order to create greater value through brand and product development."

**In plain words:** the list reads like a ladder. Type 1 does not even meet the final customer. Type 2 sells with the 4 Ps. Type 3 works with groups. Type 4 knows each individual and keeps him. Type 5 creates value together with each customer (compare slide 3: "co-create value"). The ladder picture is my reading; the slide gives only the list.

**Slide 32 ★ — Implementing CRM and overcoming barriers.** Six "Real Barriers":

1. "Corporate culture."
2. "Employee attitudes."
3. "Organizational structure."
4. "Lack of a strategic plan, quantifiable goals and objectives."
5. "Lack of integration with business unit strategies."
6. "Lack of program performance metrics."

Example on the slide: "Customer-centric orientation replaces the mass-marketing ideology. Product Sales manager vs Customer Relationship manager (do organizations have these?)"

**In plain words:** none of the six barriers is technology. Three are about people and structure. Three are things that are missing ("Lack of ...").

Opening line (no mark, but sets the scene): the architecture has five numbered components between the customers at the top and the back office at the bottom.

   - It is the customer-facing part: marketing, sales and customer service personnel. (1 mark)
   - Its technologies "include customer engagement centers, sales force automation, and the Internet". (1 mark)

Opening principle (slides 7 and 9): "Develop Relationship require resources" and "Not all customers are created equal." CRM pays only when a relationship can make customers more profitable.

   - Why: the customer wants the product, not a relationship (slide 12: "Customers do not value relationship only are transactional focused"). A better 4 Ps mix is enough.
2. **The company has no direct contact with the final customer.**
   - Source: CRM1 slide 31, type 1: "Manufacturers that have no contact with final customers at point-of-sale and focus on customer relationships with middlemen in the channel."
   - Why: CRM needs customer data "from all touch points" (slide 6). With no touch points, there is little final-customer data to collect and nobody to personalise for. The relationship that matters is with the middlemen.
3. **There is no competition and no segmentation (monopoly).**

4. **Every customer gives about the same profit (shallow value skew).**
   - Source: (from lecture CRM2/CRM3): shallow value-skewed customer bases, where cost-cutting / automation beats relationship building.
   - Why: CRM works by "differentiating profitable from unprofitable customers and segments" (CRM1 slide 14, objective 3) and by directing effort "to those with high customer lifetime values" (slide 4). If all customers are worth the same, there is no key group to find.

| A | B | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-selling | Up-selling | Cross = *other* products ("similar products, or other related / non related products"). Up = *higher* products ("more expensive, and prestige products"; "trading up"). |
| Five key processes (slides 4–6) | Five architecture components (slides 15–20) | Processes are things the company *does* (strategy, value, channels, information, assessment). Components are *parts of the system* (front office, BI, rules, links, metrics). |
| Five key processes | Six-step timeline (slides 39–43) | Processes never end. The timeline is the order of work to set CRM up. |
| Data warehouse / data mart | Data mining and intelligence tools | Warehouse and mart "merely store and update". Mining tools "segment", "attach value" and "differentiate". |
| Workflow and business rules | Physical links | Rules carry *BI* to the front office (how valuable is this customer?). Links carry *back-office* facts (is it in stock? has he paid?). |
| Selective demarketing | General demarketing | Selective = "specific consumer groups". General = "for everyone". |
| Selective demarketing | Ostensible demarketing | Selective really wants less demand from some groups. Ostensible wants *more* demand, by an "artificial shortage". |
| Operational CRM | Analytical CRM | Operational = "transactions and data collection". Analytical = "analysis of the data collected". Collect first, analyse second. |

## Memory hooks

- **Five key processes — S V M I P:** *Serve Very Many Important People* = Strategy development, Value creation, Multichannel integration, Information management, Performance assessment.
- **Architecture 1–5 — a body:** 1 face (front office meets the customer), 2 brain (BI), 3 nerves (workflow and rules carry the brain's knowledge to the face), 4 hands (back office delivers), 5 health check (metrics).
- **10 functions — the customer's life:** Identify → Acquire → Develop → Cross-sell → Up-sell → Migrate → Service → Retain → Loyalty → Win back. First letters: **I A D C U M S R L W** (*I Am Doing CRM: Up, Migrate, Serve, Retain, Loyal, Win-back*).
- **Demarketing — S O G:** Selective (some groups), Ostensible (only looks like a shortage), General (everyone).
- **De-marketing examples — M A C:** **M**inimum order sizes, **A**dmittance charges (high), **C**harges for lack of profitable usage.
- **Gartner numbers:** *a third goes old each year, a quarter is wrong* = 33 percent erodes per year, 25 percent inaccurate.
- **Six real barriers — C A S + 3 Lacks:** Culture, Attitudes, Structure; lack of Plan, lack of Integration, lack of Metrics.
- **Six-step timeline — P I C B O M:** Plan → Impact → Communicate → Build → Organize → Metrics (*Please Inform Colleagues Before Ordering Machines*).
- **Three types of CRM — O A S:** Operational collects, Analytical analyses, Social engages.
- **Decades:** M (Market research) comes before R (Relationship marketing) in the alphabet; 1980s come before 1990s.
- **Why now — four changes:** more Competitors, old Media gone, faster Pace of life, Internet and multichannel.
- **Relationship hierarchy:** strangers → acquaintances → friends → partners (each word is closer than the one before).
- **Campaign management:** plan, target, schedule, measure, then modify.

**Short questions**

1. Three approaches (slide 11):
   1. Selective demarketing — reduce demand "from specific consumer groups, to protect their well / targeted customers". (1 mark)
   2. Ostensible demarketing — "create artificial shortage of a product to increase the demand", e.g. restriction of sales, sales by invitation. (1 mark)
   3. General demarketing — demarket the product "for everyone", e.g. government demarketing of alcohol and tobacco. (1 mark)

   Common error: giving "general" for a case that targets only one group.
2. Six steps (slides 39–43), 1 mark each, order matters:
   1. "Establish a strategic plan, goals and objectives for CRM system"
   2. "Estimate the impacts on ongoing structure"
   3. "Communicate the relationship marketing philosophy from the top down"
   4. "Build the CRM platform technology"
   5. "Organize to expedite the development of the CRM system"
   6. "Establish CRM program metrics to measure the effectiveness of performance"

   Common error: putting "Build" before "Communicate". The slides communicate first.
3. Journey map and CEM (slides 46–47):
   1. A journey map describes "the steps a typical customer goes through to get something done". (1 mark)
   2. It lets "a design team ... visualize the activities"; example steps: information search, web site visit, store visit, comparing alternatives, purchase, delivery, calls. (1 mark)
   3. CEM (Gartner) = the "practice of designing and reacting to customer interactions to meet or exceed customer expectations and, thus, increase customer satisfaction, loyalty and advocacy". (1 mark)
   4. Goal of CEM = "to increase customer satisfaction, retention and loyalty so that customers become brand advocates". (1 mark)

   Common error: defining customer experience (what the customer feels) when the question asks for CEM (what the company does).
