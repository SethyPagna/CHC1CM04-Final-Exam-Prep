## The lecture in one minute

- The lecture starts with a ladder of five words: Data → Information → Knowledge → Expertise → Wisdom. Each step adds something to the step before.
- Marketing research is "the process of designing, gathering, analyzing, and reporting data and information that may be used to solve a specific marketing problem (e.g., CRM problems)".
- There are four kinds of research: product, promotion, pricing, place.
- Two slides are both called "Market Research Techniques": 4 families (slide 4) and 8 techniques (slide 6). ★★ Longitudinal studies "repeatedly measure the same sample units of a population over a period of time".
- Research follows the 11-step "Market Research Life Cycle", from "Establish the need for marketing research" to "Communicate the insights".
- Three ways of collecting data are described in detail: observation, focus groups, interviews.
- ★★ Every questionnaire question measures at one of four levels: nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio.
- Each thing you want to measure (a "construct") has a recommended scale. Brand/store image → semantic differential. Lifestyle or opinion → Likert. Performance or attitude → labelled, numbered, or Stapel (−3 to +3).

| Data | "primary, exists at a particular time and space location, no application associated" | A raw fact about one time and place. Nobody is using it yet. |
| Information | "associated with application (i.e., answer questions), either primary or derived data" | Data that answers a question. |
| Derived data | "62 marks (derived data)" | A value you calculate from other data, such as an average. |

| Neuromarketing | "Observe brain activity as consumers are exposed to stimuli such as packages or ads." | Watching the brain while a person looks at a pack or an advertisement. |
| Secondary data analysis | "Searching and interpreting existing information." | Using data that already exists. You do not collect new data. |
| Experience survey | "Gathering information from those thought to be knowledgeable on the issues relevant to the research problem." | Asking people who know the topic well (experts). |
| Case analysis | "Review previous cases." | Looking at similar past situations. |

| Experiment | "relationship between independent and dependent variables" | Change one thing (the cause) and measure what happens to another thing (the effect). |

| Ordinal measure | Heading "B. Ordinal Measure Questions" (slide 12). BI2 slide 39: "Ordinal (Ranked)" | The answers can be put in order, but the gaps are not known. |
| Interval scale | Heading "C. Interval Scale Measure Questions" (slide 13). BI2 slide 40: "the difference between any two data values is meaningful, however, there is no natural zero starting point" | Numbers with equal steps, but zero does not mean "none". |
| Ratio scale | Heading "D. Ratio Scale Measure Questions" (slide 14). BI2 slide 41: "there is also a natural zero starting point (where zero indicates that none of the quantity is present)" | Numbers with a true zero, so "twice as much" makes sense. |

## Part 1 — Data, Information, Knowledge, Expertise and Wisdom (slides 1–2)

Slide 1 is the title slide ("Lecture CRM.5").

★ Slide 2 is a prime source for multiple-choice and true/false items. The slide highlights five phrases in yellow. They are the phrases in the "Key phrase" column. Learn them word for word.

| Level | Key phrase (highlighted on the slide) | Rest of the slide wording | Slide example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Data | "no application associated" | "primary, exists at a particular time and space location" | "What is the time and date now (time location) in Hong Kong (space location). Ans:- 1:00 pm, 9th Sept 2024." "How tall are you in Feb, 2021? Ans:-170 cm." |
| 2. Information | "associated with application (i.e., answer questions)" | "either primary or derived data" | "What is the average passing mark last year in this subject? Ans:- 62 marks (derived data)." "Who has received the Dean's award? Ans:- John Wong (primary data)." |
| 3. Knowledge | "causal relationship" | "(and may be very complicated) among data / information" | "If you get 70 marks, then you will get an A. (A decision table, decision tree)." |
| 4. Expertise | "integrate huge amount of knowledge in different ways / architectures" | "(Mathematical, physical, financial, computing, medical, legal, engineering, social, psychology, political, arts and music …etc.) that can be used for knowledge intensive applications" | "E.g., ChatGPT, automatic driving, self-learning abilities." |
| 5. Wisdom | "create new knowledge" | "and / or combine them with existing models to explain and predict the future" | (no example on the slide) |

**In plain words:** Data is a raw fact with a time and a place. Information is data that somebody uses to answer a question. Knowledge is a rule that connects facts (if … then …). Expertise is a very large number of rules joined together to do difficult work. Wisdom makes new knowledge and uses it to explain and predict the future.

Two details that are easy to test:

- Information can be "either primary or derived data". The average mark (62) is derived, because it is calculated. The name John Wong is primary, because it is read directly.
- On this slide "primary" means "not calculated from other data". This is a different meaning from "primary data" in the 2023-24 matching question (see Part 3).

Example **(extra, not on the slides)**: "Shop sales on 1 Dec = $8,000" is data. "Which branch sold the most last week? Mong Kok" is information. "If it rains, then umbrella sales go up" is knowledge.

## Part 2 — What marketing research is (slide 3)

★ Definition (marked against the slide wording):

"Marketing research is the process of designing, gathering, analyzing, and reporting data and information that may be used to solve a specific marketing problem (e.g., CRM problems)."

**In plain words:** Marketing research is a planned study. It has four actions (design, gather, analyze, report). Its purpose is to solve one specific marketing problem.

|---|---|---|

| 2 | Promotion research | "The results of the advertising, sales force, publicity / PR, and promotional offers, and design next promotion mix." |
| 3 | Pricing research | "Customers perceived value?" |

**In plain words:** Product = what we sell. Promotion = how we tell people. Pricing = what the customer thinks it is worth. Place = where and through which channel we sell. These four match the marketing "4 Ps" (the slide does not use the name "4 Ps").

How to recognise the answer in a question:

- "over time", "changing", "trend of the same customers" → longitudinal studies.
- "existing information", "already collected" → secondary data analysis.
- "knowledgeable people", "experts" → experience surveys.
- "moderator", "small group", "discussion" → focus groups.
- "representative sample", "population" → surveys.
- "cause and effect", "independent and dependent variables" → experiment.

**Primary vs secondary data (2023-24 final matching).** The exam wording was: primary data = "The data that are collected for the first time"; secondary data = "The data that have already been collected for a specific purpose". This lecture's link is technique 1: "Secondary Data Analysis – Searching and interpreting existing information." Techniques that collect new data for the present study (for example observation, focus groups, surveys, longitudinal studies, experiments) produce primary data. This last sentence is my reading; the slide does not say it.

Example **(extra, not on the slides)**: a Hong Kong supermarket reads last year's sales records = secondary data. It asks 300 shoppers a new set of questions = primary data.

## Part 4 — Market Research Life Cycle (slide 7)

★ Slide 7 is a picture of 11 steps drawn as a staircase. The order is fixed. A true/false item can swap two neighbouring steps.

1. "Step 1: Establish the need for marketing research"
2. "Step 2: Define the problem"
3. "Step 3: Establish research objectives"
4. "Step 4: Determine research design"
5. "Step 5: Identify information types and sources"
6. "Step 6: Determine methods of accessing data"
7. "Step 7: Design data collection forms"
8. "Step 8: Determine the sample plan and size"
9. "Step 9: Collect data"
10. "Step 10: Analyze data"
11. "Step 11: Communicate the insights"

**In plain words:** First decide if research is needed and what the problem is (steps 1–3). Then plan how to do it (steps 4–8). Then do it and report it (steps 9–11).

Order traps:

- The problem (step 2) comes before the objectives (step 3).
- The forms (step 7) come before the sample plan and size (step 8).
- The last step is "Communicate the insights", not "Analyze data".

## Part 5 — Observation, focus groups and interviews (slides 8–10)

### Slide 8 — Observation ★

1. "Direct observation - in real time, and everything."
2. "Indirect observation - only the effects or results of the behavior will be observed."
3. "Object's awareness of being observed or not."
4. "For short time interval only."
5. "Only observe public behavior."
6. "Advantages"
   - "Natural behavior."
   - "Real data, can be used to cross check with other collected data (from interviews)."
7. "Dis-Advantages"
   - "Small amount of data, may not be representatives."
   - "Objects may act differently when they know that they are under observed."

**In plain words:** Direct observation = you watch the behaviour while it happens. Indirect observation = you do not see the behaviour; you see only what it leaves behind. The person may or may not know that somebody is watching (point 3). You can watch only for a short time (point 4) and only in public places (point 5).

Example **(extra, not on the slides)**: watching shoppers choose drinks in a store is direct. Counting the empty shelf spaces at closing time is indirect.

1. "Encourage openness."
2. "Generate new ideas."
3. "Deep interactions possible."
4. "Very effective at delivering actionable insights."
5. "On-line focus groups possible."

   - "Are they constituting representative samples?"

**In plain words:** A focus group makes people speak freely and gives new ideas that the company can act on. It can also be held online. Before you run one, you must answer six set-up questions: how many people, who, how many groups, how to choose them, whether they represent the population, and how to find an experienced moderator. 1. "One to one questions and answers."
2. "Open questions mainly."
3. "Unrestricted comments and opinions."
4. "Personal feelings and relationships information with the company may be easier to obtained."
5. "Deep interactions possible."
6. "Dis-Advantages"

   - "Time consuming."

## Part 6 — Questionnaires: the four levels of measurement (slides 11–14)

★★ The 2025-26 final Q21(a) (4 marks) asked: "Identify the level of measurement used in each of the following. Please also tell whether it is discrete or continuous if it is quantitative data". Slides 11–14 give one group of example questions for each level. The definitions of the levels are in lecture BI2 (slides 38–41).

### How to decide the level of measurement

Ask these questions in order. Stop at the first "yes, and nothing more".

1. Can you only **name** the answer (no order)? → **nominal**.
2. Can you put the answers in **order**, but the gaps are not known? → **ordinal**.
3. Are the **differences** between values meaningful, but there is **no true zero**? → **interval**.
4. Is there a **true zero** (zero = none of the quantity), so that **ratios** make sense ("twice as much")? → **ratio**.
5. Only for interval and ratio (quantitative data): was the value **counted** → **discrete**; was it **measured** → **continuous**. For nominal and ordinal write "not applicable (qualitative data)".

Step 5 uses BI2 slide 38: discrete data "usually result from counting"; continuous data "usually result from measuring".

Quick test for step 4: "Is 20 twice as much as 10?" A person aged 40 is twice as old as a person aged 20 → ratio. 20 degrees is not twice as hot as 10 degrees → interval.

## Part 7 — Questionnaires: rating-scale formats (slides 15–17)

These three slides are pictures with no heading that names a level. The classifications below are mine. They follow the rule of slide 13 (a balanced rating scale is shown as an interval scale question) and the warning in Part 6.

### Slide 15 — three 7-point labelled scales ★

The slide shows an arrow from "Extremely Negative" through "Neutral" to "Extremely Positive". Under the arrow are three scales. Each has 7 points and a neutral middle point.

| Point | Agreement scale | Satisfaction scale | Likelihood scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (extremely negative) | Strongly disagree | Extremely dissatisfied | Extremely unlikely |
| 2 | Disagree | Moderately dissatisfied | Moderately unlikely |
| 3 | Somewhat disagree | Slightly dissatisfied | Slightly unlikely |
| 4 (neutral) | Neither agree nor disagree | Neither satisfied nor dissatisfied | Neither likely nor unlikely |
| 5 | Somewhat agree | Slightly satisfied | Slightly likely |
| 6 | Agree | Moderately satisfied | Moderately likely |
| 7 (extremely positive) | Strongly agree | Extremely Satisfied | Extremely likely |

**In plain words:** All three scales are balanced: three negative points, one neutral point in the middle, three positive points.

Level: the same form as slide 13 question C2 → **interval** in this lecture; **ordinal** by BI2 slide 39.

### Slide 16 — a Likert example ★

"Indicate the degree to which you agree or disagree with each of the following statements." The 7 columns run from "Strongly disagree" to "Strongly agree" (the same 7 labels as the agreement scale above). The five statements are:

1. "Levi's 501 jeans are good looking."
2. "Levi's 501 jeans are reasonably priced."
3. "Your next pair of jeans will be Levi's 501 jeans."
4. "Levi's 501 jeans are easy to identify on someone."
5. "Levi's 501 jeans make you feel good."

**In plain words:** This is the Likert format: a list of statements and one agree–disagree scale for all of them.

Level: **interval** in this lecture (same as C2); **ordinal** by BI2 slide 39.

## Part 8 — Which scale for which construct (slides 18–19)

★ A "construct" is the thing you want to measure. Slides 18 and 19 are one table with seven constructs. This table is a likely source for "which scale would you use" items.

| # | Construct | Recommended scale (slide wording) | Example on the slide |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Brand/store image | "Semantic differential (with 5 or 7 scale points) using a set of bipolar adjectives" | "Refer to example on page 198." (a textbook page; no example is printed here. The Chevrolet Camaro question on slide 13 has this format.) |
| 2 | Frequency of use | "Labeled (Never, Rarely, Sometimes, Often, Quite often, Very often) OR number of times per relevant time period (e.g., month)" | "How often do you buy delivered Chinese dinners?" |
| 3 | Importance | "Labeled (Unimportant, Slightly important, Important, Quite important, Very important) OR numbered rating using 5 scale points" | "How important is it to you that your dry-cleaning service has same-day service?" |
| 4 | Intention to purchase | "Labeled (Very unlikely, Unlikely, Somewhat unlikely, Undecided, Somewhat likely, Likely, Very likely) OR 100% probability" | "The next time you buy cookies, how likely are you to buy a fat-free brand?" |
| 5 | Lifestyle or opinion | "Likert (Strongly disagree–Strongly agree with 5 or 7 scale points) using a series of lifestyle statements" | "Indicate how much you agree or disagree with each of the following statements." 1. "I have a busy schedule." 2. "I work a great deal." |
| 6 | Performance or attitude | "Labeled (Poor, Fair, Good, Very good, Excellent) OR numbered rating scale using 5 or 7 scale points OR Stapel scale using −3 to +3" | "Indicate how well you think Arby's performs on each of the following features." 1. "Variety of items on the menu" 2. "Reasonable price" 3. "Location convenient to your home" |
| 7 | Satisfaction | "Labeled (Very satisfied, Satisfied, Somewhat satisfied, Neutral, Somewhat unsatisfied, Unsatisfied, Very unsatisfied) OR 10-point satisfaction scale where 1 = “Not at all satisfied” and 10 = “Completely satisfied”" | "Based on your experience with Federal Express, how satisfied have you been with its" (the sentence is cut off on the slide) |

**In plain words:**

- Image of a brand or shop → pairs of opposite words (semantic differential).
- How often → frequency words, or a real count per month.
- How important → importance words, or 1 to 5.
- Will you buy → likelihood words, or a probability out of 100%.
- Lifestyle or opinion → statements with agree–disagree (Likert).
- How well something performs → quality words (Poor … Excellent), a numbered rating, or Stapel (−3 to +3).
- Satisfaction → satisfaction words, or 1 to 10.

Link to Part 6 (my link, not on the slides): "number of times per relevant time period" is a count, so it gives ratio data, like slide 14 question D2. The labelled choices give ordered categories.

### Model answers

**Q1 (3 marks). Define marketing research.**

1. It is "the process of designing, gathering, analyzing, and reporting" (1 mark)
2. "data and information" (1 mark)
3. "that may be used to solve a specific marketing problem (e.g., CRM problems)". (1 mark)

**Q2 (5 marks). Distinguish data, information, knowledge, expertise and wisdom.**

1. Data: "exists at a particular time and space location, no application associated", e.g. height 170 cm in Feb 2021. (1 mark)
2. Information: "associated with application (i.e., answer questions)", primary or derived, e.g. average passing mark 62. (1 mark)
3. Knowledge: "causal relationship" among data / information, e.g. if you get 70 marks, then you get an A. (1 mark)
4. Expertise: "integrate huge amount of knowledge in different ways / architectures" for knowledge intensive applications, e.g. ChatGPT, automatic driving. (1 mark)
5. Wisdom: "create new knowledge" and / or combine it with existing models "to explain and predict the future". (1 mark)

**Q3 (4 marks). 2025-26 final Q21(a): identify the level of measurement; say discrete or continuous if quantitative.**

1. Eye colours of students: nominal; not applicable (qualitative).
2. Pulse rates of male students: ratio; discrete (beats per minute are counted).
3. Body temperatures in degrees: interval; continuous (no true zero; measured).
4. Ages in years of homes sold: ratio; continuous (age is a measured quantity).
5. Age brackets (under 30 / 30–49 / 50–64 / over 64): ordinal; not applicable (qualitative).

The paper gave 4 marks for this part. The split per item is not known. Give the level and the discrete/continuous answer for every item, each with a few words of reason.

**Q4 (assume 4 marks). Where can we get customer-related data for analytical CRM?** (2023-24 final. This answer is a synthesis; no single slide answers the question. Give four named sources; the fifth is a safety point.)

1. Touch points: data recorded at every point of contact between the customer and the company, such as purchases, web site, mobile app, call centre and social media. CRM1 defines a touch point as "any point of contact that a customer or prospect has with the company". (1 mark)
2. Secondary data analysis: "searching and interpreting existing information", for example the company's own past records. (1 mark)
3. Surveys and questionnaires: samples "drawn so that they are representatives of a specific population". (1 mark)
4. Qualitative research: focus groups and interviews with customers. (1 mark)
5. Observation: "people, objects, and/or activities are systematically observed and documented"; or longitudinal studies of the same customers over time. (1 mark)

**Q5 (4 marks). Explain direct and indirect observation. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of observation.**

1. Direct observation: "in real time, and everything". (1 mark)
2. Indirect observation: "only the effects or results of the behavior will be observed". (1 mark)
3. Advantage: "Natural behavior", or real data that can be used to cross check other collected data (from interviews). (1 mark)
4. Disadvantage: "Small amount of data, may not be representatives", or objects may act differently when they know they are observed. (1 mark)

**Q6 (4 marks). Which scale would you use to measure (a) store image, (b) customer lifestyle, (c) restaurant performance, (d) satisfaction?**

1. (a) Store image: semantic differential with 5 or 7 scale points, "using a set of bipolar adjectives". (1 mark)
2. (b) Lifestyle: Likert, "Strongly disagree–Strongly agree with 5 or 7 scale points", using a series of lifestyle statements. (1 mark)
3. (c) Performance: labelled (Poor, Fair, Good, Very good, Excellent), or a numbered rating scale with 5 or 7 points, or a Stapel scale using −3 to +3. (1 mark)
4. (d) Satisfaction: labelled (Very satisfied … Very unsatisfied), or a 10-point scale where 1 = "Not at all satisfied" and 10 = "Completely satisfied". (1 mark)

## Easy to mix up

| A | B | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|---|
| Data | Information | Data has "no application associated". Information answers a question. |
| Information | Knowledge | Information is one answer (62 marks). Knowledge is a rule: if 70 marks, then A. |
| Expertise | Wisdom | Expertise integrates existing knowledge (ChatGPT). Wisdom creates new knowledge and predicts the future. |
| "Primary" on slide 2 | "Primary data" in the 2023-24 matching question | Slide 2: primary = not derived (John Wong vs the calculated 62). Matching question: primary = "collected for the first time", the opposite of secondary data. |
| Slide 4 list | Slide 6 list | Same title. Slide 4 has 4 families (qualitative, mobile and social media, sampling, neuromarketing). Slide 6 has 8 techniques. |
| Experience surveys | Surveys | Experience surveys ask knowledgeable people. Surveys ask a representative sample of a population. |
| Surveys | Longitudinal studies | A survey draws a representative sample. A longitudinal study measures the same sample units repeatedly over time. |
| Secondary data analysis | Case analysis | Secondary = existing information of any kind. Case analysis = previous cases. |
| Direct observation | Indirect observation | Direct = real time, everything. Indirect = only the effects or results. |
| Observation | Neuromarketing | Observation records people, objects and activities. Neuromarketing observes brain activity. |
| Focus group | Interview | Focus group = small group + moderator + unstructured discussion. Interview = one to one, open questions. "Deep interactions possible" is on both slides. |
| Likert | Semantic differential | Likert = statements + agree/disagree (lifestyle or opinion). Semantic differential = bipolar adjectives (brand/store image). |
| Semantic differential | Stapel | Semantic differential has two opposite adjectives. Stapel uses numbers −3 to +3 and is listed under performance or attitude. |
| Ordinal | Interval | Ordinal = rank only (1st, 2nd, 3rd). Interval = equal steps (a 1–10 rating on slide 13). |
| Interval | Ratio | Interval has no true zero (temperature in degrees). Ratio has a true zero (age, number of purchases). |
| Discrete | Continuous | Counted → discrete. Measured → continuous. Only for interval and ratio data. |
| Qualitative research (slide 4) | Qualitative data (BI2) | Slide 4: a research family (focus groups, interviews, shopping with consumers). BI2: nominal and ordinal data. |
| Likert in CRM5 slide 13 | Likert in BI2 slide 39 | CRM5 shows it as an interval scale question. BI2 lists it as ordinal. Follow the wording of the question. |

## Memory hooks

- **Five levels: D-I-K-E-W.** "**D**id **I** **K**now **E**verything? **W**ow." One key word each: no application → answers questions → causal → integrate → create new.
- **Four research types = 4 Ps:** Product, Promotion, Pricing, Place.
- **Four families: Q-M-S-N.** **Q**uestion people (qualitative), **M**obile, **S**ample, **N**euro (brain).
- **Eight techniques: "Some Experts Can Observe Focus groups, Survey Long Experiments."** Secondary, Experience, Case, Observation, Focus groups, Surveys, Longitudinal, Experiment.
- **Eleven steps = 3 + 5 + 3.** WHY (need, problem, objectives) → HOW (design, information types and sources, methods of accessing data, forms, sample plan and size) → DO (collect, analyze, communicate).
- **Levels: N-O-I-R** (the French word for "black"). Each level adds one thing: **N**ame → **O**rder → equal **I**ntervals → t**R**ue zero. This order is also the order of slides 11, 12, 13, 14 (A, B, C, D).
- **Count → disCrete. Measure → continuous.**
- **Observation limits: A-S-P.** **A**wareness, **S**hort time interval, **P**ublic behaviour.
- **Interview disadvantages: C-T-E-S-H.** **C**ostly, **T**ime consuming, **E**xperienced interviewer difficult to recruit, **S**mall sample size, **H**onest answers doubtful.
- **Seven constructs: "Brand Fans Insist I Love Perfect Service."** Brand/store image, Frequency of use, Importance, Intention to purchase, Lifestyle or opinion, Performance or attitude, Satisfaction.
- **Scale pairs:** **L**ifestyle → **L**ikert. Image → two opposite adjectives (semantic differential). Performance → Stapel is allowed (−3 to +3).
