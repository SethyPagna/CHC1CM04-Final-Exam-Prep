## 3. What the past papers tell us

I read three past finals and one past mid-term (with its official marking scheme).

| Paper | Structure | Long questions |
|---|---|---|
| 2025-26 final (3 hours) | 20 MCQ + 5 long questions (80 marks) | Q21 levels of measurement + frequency table (10) · Q22 paired t-test (10) · Q23 ID3 decision tree (20) · Q24 Apriori (25) · Q25 customer segment profit, Pareto, CRM (15) |
| 2024-25 final (2 hours) | 20 MCQ (40%) + 4 long questions | Q21 four situations where CRM investment is not worthwhile (10) · Q22 CRM architecture (15) · Q23 mean, median, mode, quartiles, SD of 27 ages (15) · Q24 ID3 decision tree (20) |
| 2023-24 final (older syllabus, other lecturer) | MCQ, fill-in, matching, short, long | CRM project vs process · data-mining applications · star schema · cluster profiling. Mostly outside this year's slides. |
| Mid-term 2024 (with marking scheme) | 10 MCQ + 4 questions | CRM definition and five processes · Pareto, 150–20 rule, tiered servicing, de-marketing · bank tiers and retention · CPV steps, positioning map |

Four patterns:

1. **About 65–80% of the long-question marks came from BI2–BI5** (statistics and data mining). In 2025-26 it was 65 of 80.
2. **The ID3 decision tree came two years in a row, 20 marks each time.** It is the safest bet in the course.
3. **Each calculation question gives a small table and asks for a method you can do by hand in 10–20 minutes.**
4. **The marking scheme gives one mark per correct point.** A 5-mark answer needs five short, separate points.

What is new this year: a True/False part, and a split into short questions (about 30 marks) and long questions (about 35 marks). So expect fewer giant questions and more 4–6 mark questions.

## 6. How to answer each part

**Time plan for 3 hours (100 marks, about 1.7 minutes per mark, 10 minutes spare):**

| Part | Marks | Time |
|---|---|---|
| A — MCQ | about 25 | 35 minutes |
| B — True/False | about 10 | 10 minutes |
| C — short questions | about 30 | 55 minutes |
| D — long questions | about 35 | 65 minutes |
| Checking | — | 15 minutes |

Start with Part D if calculations are your strong side. The marks there are the easiest to secure with a method.

**MCQ.** Read the last line of the question first. Cross out the two options that are clearly wrong. Never leave a blank.

**True/False.** One wrong word makes the whole statement false. Look for swapped pairs (Type I and Type II, stratified and cluster, support and confidence) and for absolute words ("always", "never", "only", "guarantees").

**Short questions.** Count the marks. Write that many numbered points. For "explain", write the term, what it means, and one example.

**Calculations.** Always write these four lines, even when you use the calculator:

1. The formula in symbols.
2. The numbers put into the formula.
3. The answer, rounded as the paper says (the 2025-26 paper said 2 decimal places). Keep 3–4 decimals in the middle steps.
4. One sentence that says what the answer means for the business.

Line 4 is where students lose marks. "Reject H0" is not a conclusion. "The training program significantly increased the test scores" is.

## 7. Calculator notes

- log base 2: `log₂ x = log x ÷ log 2`. For entropy, the cheat sheet has a table of ready values, so you often do not need the log key at all.
- Standard deviation: use the **sample** key (marked `xσn−1` or `sx`), not `xσn`. The slides divide by n − 1.
- Many HKEAA-approved Casio models (fx-50FH, fx-3650P) have an SD mode for mean and SD, and a REG (Lin) mode that gives A (the intercept b0) and B (the slope b1). Try it on your own calculator this week with the regression example in note 09 (answer: b1 = 0.9286, b0 = 4.2). If your model does not have it, the table method in the note takes 5 minutes.
- Use the calculator to check. Still write the formula and the sums on the paper, because the marks are for the steps.
